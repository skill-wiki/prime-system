# Founder Prose

Start from one judgment the author is willing to own. Gather the evidence,
counterevidence, dates, measurements, and unresolved questions before outlining.
Do not create an industry-trend introduction merely because the page is long.

## Keep voice and facts separate

Voice samples control rhythm, vocabulary, and how the author makes a judgment.
Content sources control every technical claim. Never import facts from a style
sample or imitate a third party's distinctive voice.

When the author has not supplied voice samples, write a restrained factual draft
and mark the few places where an interview answer would materially change it.
Do not invent personal experience, user quotes, emotions, or chronology.

Read [references/source-notes.md](references/source-notes.md) before turning a
large implementation history into an essay or launch note.

## Shape the argument

- Open with the concrete contradiction, discovery, or result.
- Explain what was believed, what evidence broke that belief, and what changed.
- Preserve uncertainty when the evidence is incomplete.
- Use first person only for choices, observations, and work the author can own.
- Prefer named files, commands, counts, and failures over adjectives.
- End at the consequence or next decision. Do not repeat the thesis as a slogan.

Remove worklog residue that does not help the reader: lane numbers, agent roles,
internal approval choreography, or repeated proof that every task was attempted.
Keep failures and reversals when they explain the architecture.
